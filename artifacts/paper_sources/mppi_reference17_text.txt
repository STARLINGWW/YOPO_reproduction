Flight With Limited Field of View: A Parallel and
Gradient-Free Strategy for Micro Aerial Vehicle
Hanchen Lu

Abstract—In this article, a parallel and gradient-free strat-
egy is presented for autonomous navigation of a quadrotor
with a limited ﬁeld of view (FOV). The FOV constraint is non-
linear and noncontinuous in general and involves higher
order dynamics of the vehicle. Planning problems with such
constraints are difﬁcult to solve, especially with the real-
time requirement. The proposed method ﬁrst constructs
an environment map by integrating the measurement of a
limited-FOV sensor in a parallel process. Based on the map,
it then selects a feasible local goal from a global path and
generates a constraint-free nominal control sequence. Fi-
nally, a stochastic optimal control problem is solved to sat-
isfy the limited FOV constraint, the dynamic constraint, and
the collision-free conditions at the same time. The objective
functions are nonlinear and noncontinuous and involves
dynamic of MAV. A parallel-gradient-free algorithm is used
to address these problems. Both the sensing and planning
problems are solved parallelly on a lightweight graphics
processing unit. The effectiveness of the proposed strat-
egy is demonstrated through both simulation and real-ﬂight
experiments.
Index Terms—Collision avoidance, motion planning, nav-
igation, parallel processing, unmanned aerial vehicles.

I. INTRODUCTION
W
ITH its superior mobility, micro aerial vehicles, espe-
cially quadrotors, are ideal for a wide range of applica-
tions. Thanks to the advancement of sensors and computational
hardware, intelligent algorithms can now be implemented to
navigate the vehicle through challenging obstacle-strewn en-
vironment. However, due to the nonconvex and noncontinuous
nature of the problem, ﬂight in unknown and cluttered envi-
ronments with a limited ﬁeld of view (FOV) still remains a
challenge.
For a rigidly connected visual perception system, the per-
ception capability is strongly affected by both the environment
and the platform’s motion. To ensure safety at a given time
instance, the position of the vehicle must be conﬁned by the
volume of FOV from a previous moment. Together with the
effect of obstacleocclusion, thenonconvexandtemporal-limited
FOV constraint poses challenges for most existing methods.
In [1]–[3], the feasible trajectory is limited to an explored
known space. This conservative constraint prevents the vehicle
from achieving agile movement and assumes a largely static
environment. The method in [4] and [5] plans an additional
safe-stopping trajectory for all potential motion. It ensures the
vehicle to stop inside the known free space at any moment.
Zhou et al. [6] adopted an iterative risk-aware reﬁnement to
postprocess the trajectory and plans the yaw movement in a
separate stage. The awareness is introduced through optimizing
an indirect visibility metric with a series of geometrical criteria,
making the algorithm less intuitive in general. On the other hand,
Liu et al. [7] proposed a search-based framework to directly
penalize and satisfy the FOV constraint. Though the approach
is straightforward, real-time performance is difﬁcult to obtain
due to the high degree of freedom of the search problem. To
address the issue, our approach adopts the stochastic optimal
control framework [8] to formulate a model predictive control
(MPC) problem that can be solved with an efﬁcient gradient-free
sampling-based method [9], [10].
In order to better use the limited FOV sensor, its reading
is often fused into a single-environment map. Many meth-
ods [11], [12] also require additional information about the
environment, such as the distance ﬁeld, which is the distance
from a given point to its closest obstacle. Popular methods
like [13] construct the world model by performing ray-casting
and a wave-front-propagation-based distance ﬁeld calculation.
Their computational complexity increases with the resolution of
the depth information and with the number of sensed obstacle

1) The proposed approach directly penalizes and satisﬁes

II. RELATED WORK
Motion planning for quadrotors has been extensively stud-
ied. One of the earliest work [14] proposes the method of
minimum-snap trajectory generation, where the trajectory is
represented as piecewise polynomials and generated by solving
a quadratic programming problem. Richter et al. [15], [16]
showed that minimum snap trajectories can be obtained in closed
form, whereas safety of the trajectories is ensured by iteratively
inserting intermediate waypoints in an ad hoc approach. The
problem of trajectory generation with obstacle avoidance is nat-
urally nonconvex and is difﬁcult to solve. Chen et al. [17]–[20]
adopted the idea of safe-ﬂying corridor. These methods extract
an obstacle-free region consists of multiple convex shapes and
constraint the trajectory to stay inside. The resulting problem is
convex and of a quadratic form, which can be solved efﬁciently
in real time. On the other hand, works like [12], [21]–[25]
utilized a reduced model to ﬁnd a good-enough trajectory as an
initial guess, which is then reﬁned by a nonlinear optimization
process.
Compared to general motion planning, less attention was
given to the problem of safe navigation with limited perception.
In this case, an additional step is required to construct a repre-
sentation of the surrounding environment. Unlike in the cases
for scene reconstruction, the representation used for motion
planning must be updated in real time to capture the change
of the perceived environment. Popular methods like [13], [26],
ﬁrst construct a grid-based occupancy map by performing ray-
casting between the sensor to the perceived point cloud. Then,
a wave-propagation-based algorithm is adopted to calculate the
estimated distance ﬁeld. However, the time complexity of these
algorithms varies with the resolution of the depth information
and the number of the perceived obstacles. It might even require
a downsampling of the original depth information to achieve
real-time performance. In [27], an efﬁcient point cloud-based
map is proposed, but it only remembers a limited number of
frames of the perceived information, limiting its application to
the more general problem.
With a proper environmental representation, safe planning
with limited FOV is made possible. However, the optimality
and availability of the solution could be affected by this overly
conservative constraint. In [4] and [5], a high-speed trajectory
generation method was proposed that enables fast and safe
ﬂying while new environmental information was obtained. The
safety is guaranteed by always having a feasible, safe back-up
trajectory in the known free space. For navigation autonomously
in unknown environments, Zhou et al. [6] presented a robust and
perception-aware replanning framework, where an iterative tra-
jectory reﬁnement process ensures that unknown obstacles can
be observed earlier. The awareness is introduced through an in-
direct visibility metric and the heading is planned independently
of the translational motion. To address the issue, a gradient-free
optimization (GFO) framework could be considered. Successful
implementation of GFO on robotics system can be seen in [8]
and [10], where a learned vehicle model with noisy gradient is
successfully controlled. Similarly, in [28], the GFO is used to
optimize the parameters from a learned latent space for real-
time vision-based arm maneuvers. In this article, the proposed
method extends the work in [8] to formulate the limited-FOV
planning as a stochastic optimal control problem, where an
efﬁcient gradient-free-sampling-based solution applies.
III. PROBLEM DEFINITION
A. Method Overview
In this article, an MPC-based planning framework is adopted
to solve the nonconvex and noncontinuous problem in real time.
First, a dense environment map is constructed with the accumu-
lated sensor readings. Then, a global geometrical path planner
is adopted to provide guidance information and initialization to
the MPC. Finally, the MPC module serves as the local planner to
generates a safe and feasible trajectory. Mapping and planning
modules are fully parallel and implemented on a GPU. The
notation used throughout the article is shown in Fig. 1(a).

sensor provided by an external localization process. The purpose
of the map is to help evaluate various costs and constraints in
the planning phase. The proposed mapping module updates the
occupancy probability (the chance of this grid being occupied

The measurement of the depth sensor is typically in the format
of a depth image or a point cloud. By casting a ray from the
sensor’s position to each of the measured points, the grids can
be classiﬁed as free, obstacle-occupied, and not-observable [see
Fig. 2(a)]. The traditional updating process [13], [26] starts from
the grid where the sensor resides (Grid 1) and repeatedly visits
the next grid that the ray would pass through (Grids 1–8). It is
noted that when the depth sensor has a high resolution, a grid
might be updated by multiple rays (i.e., Grid 2) and cause a
decrease in the computational efﬁciency. When the ray-casting
is conducted parallelly, this leads to a memory conﬂict with
additional overhead.
Following [30], the issue can be addressed by reversely
projecting a grid to the sensor’s imaging plane. Given a grid
[see the grid under processing in Fig. 2(b)], the algorithm ﬁrst
ﬁnds its distance to the sensor, called the projected depth. The
distance between the measurement point of the closest ray and
the sensor is called the measured depth. For a sensor with a ﬁxed
measurement model, the query of the closest ray can often be
done in O(1) time. If the distance between the center of the grid
to its closest ray is larger than a threshold, then no update shall
be performed. Otherwise, the projected depth is compared to
the measured depth, and update the information of the grid as
follows.
1) If the difference is within a threshold, the grid is treated
as occupied and its occupancy probability is increased.
Fig. 3.
Rolling map mechanism.
2) If the projected depth is smaller than the measured depth,
the grid belongs to the known free space, and its occu-
pancy probability is reduced.
3) Otherwise, if the projected depth is larger than the mea-
sured depth, then the grid is occluded by an obstacle and
no updating is performed.
Since both the backward projection and the updating process
does not introduce a memory writing conﬂict, the algorithm can
safely assign a unique thread to process each of the grid cells,
which enables large-scale parallel implementation on the GPU.
Due to the mobility of the aerial vehicle, the mapping process
would consume a large amount of memory space. To address
this issue, a rolling-buffer-like strategy [11] is adopted to reuse
a ﬁnite amount of memory. The algorithm is more suitable for
the lightweight-GPU-based computer, where a limited amount
of memory has to be shared by both the GPU and CPU. Each
grid in the map is assigned with a unique global index [i, j, k],
where i, j, k ∈Z. To store the information of each grid, the 3-D
index is mapped into a 1-D index through a rolling formula

the drone is automatically forgotten and replaced by the map
information that is close to the vehicle.
The distance from a given grid to its nearest obstacle is
obtained from the Euclidean distance transform of the grid map.
To implement this process on the GPU, the algorithm in [31]
is adopted to perform a 1-D scan for each roll of the grid map
along its x-, y-, and z-axis. All rolls along the same axis can be
processed parallelly without memory conﬂict.

local planner is used to generate smooth, safe, and dynamically

IX. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
In this article, a parallel and gradient-free approach was
proposed for quadrotor ﬂying with the limited FOV. A parallel
mapping process was explored to integrate the onboard limited
FOV sensor’s measurement to an environment map. Based on
the map, a guiding strategy was adopted for guiding the local
planner. In order to satisfy the perception constraint, the de-
tailed vehicle dynamic and the collision avoidance conditions, a
stochastic optimal control problem with nonlinear and noncon-
tinuous costs was solvedusingaparallel gradient-freealgorithm.
Both the environment perception and the optimization process
were implemented on a microsized quadrotor with a lightweight
low-cost GPU. Comparisons and autonomous ﬂight experiments
were conducted to validate the proposed method.

trol, Nanjing University of Science and Technol-
ogy. Since 1992, he has been with the School of
Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Nanyang Technological Univer-
sity, Singapore, where he is currently a Professor and the Director of
the Delta-NTU Corporate Laboratory for Cyber-Physical Systems. From
July 2011 to 2014, he was the Head of the Division of Control and
Instrumentation. His research interests include robust control and es-
timation, networked control systems, multiagent networks, localization,
and unmanned systems.
Dr. Xie’s is an Editor-in-Chief of the Unmanned Systems and an
Associate Editor for the IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON NETWORK CONTROL
SYSTEMS. He was the Editor of the IET Book Series in Control and an
Associate Editor for a number of journals, including the IEEE TRANS-
ACTIONS ON AUTOMATIC CONTROL, Automatica, IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON
CONTROL SYSTEMS TECHNOLOGY, and IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON CIRCUITS
AND SYSTEMS II. He is an Elected Member of Board of Governors of the
IEEE Control System Society (January 2016–December 2018). He is a
Fellow of the IFAC.
